Idli

Description

Idli is a soft, fluffy South Indian steamed cake made from fermented rice and urad dal (black gram) batter. It’s one of the healthiest and most beloved breakfast dishes in India — light on the stomach, naturally gluten-free, and rich in protein.

The beauty of idli lies in its simplicity. The mild flavor pairs beautifully with spicy chutneys, sambar, or even a drizzle of ghee and podi. Whether served at home, in temples, or at roadside tiffin stalls, idlis are a comforting symbol of South Indian cuisine and everyday nourishment.

Ingredients

For the Batter:

  • 2 cups idli rice (or parboiled rice)
  • 1 cup urad dal (split black gram, skinless)
  • 1 teaspoon fenugreek seeds (optional, for better fermentation)
  • Salt to taste
  • Water as needed

For Steaming:

  • Oil or ghee (for greasing the idli plates)

Steps

  1. Rinse rice and urad dal separately. Soak the rice with fenugreek seeds in one bowl and the dal in another, both for about 4–6 hours.
  2. Drain the soaked water. First, grind the urad dal with a little fresh water into a smooth, fluffy paste. Then grind the rice into a slightly coarse batter. Combine both in a large bowl and mix well.
  3. Cover the bowl and let it ferment overnight or for 8–10 hours in a warm place until the batter rises and becomes airy.
  4. Gently stir the fermented batter, add salt, and mix. Grease idli moulds with oil or ghee.
  5. Pour the batter into moulds and steam in an idli steamer or pressure cooker (without the whistle) for 10–12 minutes.
  6. Insert a toothpick — it should come out clean. Remove the idlis carefully with a spoon after a few minutes of cooling.
  7. Serve hot with coconut chutney, sambar, and a drizzle of ghee if desired.
